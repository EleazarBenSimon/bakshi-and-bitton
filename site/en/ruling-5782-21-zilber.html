HCJ 5782/21

Zilber et al. v. Minister of Finance

Unelected justices voided an elected minister's budget call. Invoking the elastic "reasonableness" ground, the Supreme Court (unanimous, three-justice panel, Justice Stein writing) forced Finance Minister Lieberman to restore daycare subsidies for yeshiva-student families through the end of the academic year, deeming the mid-year cancellation without a transition period "unreasonable." Spending priorities thus shifted from the executive's hands to the bench's.

3 justicesVote 3–0Lead opinion: Alex Stein
Why this matters
Here reasonableness reached the budget table. Three unelected justices — unanimously, in Justice Stein's opinion — held that an elected finance minister acted unlawfully by cancelling a public allocation mid-year without a transition period, and compelled him to restore it through the end of the academic year. No statute was tested, no constitutional question decided — the very prioritizing of public spending, the core discretion of the executive, was drawn into a "reasonableness" standard the Court fashioned for itself. The call over who receives a subsidy from the state budget, and for how long, passed from the elected minister to the bench.
