Partially struck

HCJ 4374/15

Movement for Quality (Abolition of) Government in Israel et al. v. The Prime Minister et al.

An unelected court overturned the elected government's signature gas policy: it struck down the 'stability clause' in the gas framework — which guaranteed offshore-gas operators a decade of regulatory stability — and thereby left the entire framework inoperative. The asserted ground: the Cabinet had exceeded its authority by binding a future legislature through a commitment not anchored in statute. President Naor authored the lead opinion.

5 justicesLead opinion: Miriam Naor

HCJ 4374/15 is a ruling of the Supreme Court of Israel, decided 2016-03-27, by a panel of 5 justices. Outcome: Partially struck.

Why this matters
Here the power reached the elected government's economic policy. The Court struck the 'stability clause' at the heart of the gas framework — the commitment the whole arrangement rested on — and thereby left the government's signature energy policy inoperative, on the ground that the Cabinet had exceeded its authority by binding a future legislature through a commitment not anchored in statute. A strategic economic decision the elected executive had hammered out with the gas companies was drawn to a bench no one elected, under President Naor's lead opinion. The framing cuts both ways: in the name of protecting a future legislature's freedom, the Court took today's decision out of the elected branch's hands.
