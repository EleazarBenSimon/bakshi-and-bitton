Declarative

HCJ 156/75

Fawzi Dakka v. Minister of Transportation

The petition was dismissed unanimously by the three-justice panel — but in the same breath the Supreme Court, sitting as the High Court of Justice, handed itself a new power. Justice Meir Shamgar's lead opinion coined reasonableness for the first time as a standalone ground of review: no longer a clue to some other defect, but an open-ended license for unelected justices to strike down the elected executive's decisions by their own measure of what is "reasonable." The door Shamgar opened was thrown wide by Aharon Barak in HCJ 389/80 Dapei Zahav v. Broadcasting Authority (1980). P"D 30(2) 94.

3 justicesVote 3–0Lead opinion: Meir Shamgar

HCJ 156/75 is a ruling of the Supreme Court of Israel, decided 1976-01-01, by a panel of 3 justices. Outcome: Declarative. Vote: 3–0.

Why this matters
This is where the seed was planted. The petition was dismissed unanimously — nothing was struck, and no one was harmed enough to contest the innovation itself — yet in the same breath Justice Shamgar articulated unreasonableness for the first time as a standalone ground of judicial review: no longer a clue to some other defect, but a yardstick by which unelected justices weigh the executive's discretion against their own sense of what is "reasonable." The door that opened quietly here, in a case where the doctrine was never actually applied, was thrown wide in HCJ 389/80 Dapei Zahav — and from it every later annulment of an administrative decision in the name of reasonableness draws its authority. A tool the Court fashioned for itself, above the elected branch, that no one legislated.

Official source (print only): פ"ד ל(2) 94 (1976)

Notes

Pre-digital ruling: the 1976 judgment was published in the official law reports (פ"ד ל(2) 94) and predates the Supreme Court's free online corpus (which begins ~1986). Its full Hebrew text is digitized in the commercial Nevo database (paywalled) but is NOT on the free court.gov.il corpus. The verbatim Hebrew passage in notes_he is transcribed from the official report (vol. 30(2), p. 101) and is public domain under the Israeli Copyright Act 2007 §6 (no copyright in judicial decisions). In Justice Shamgar's words: statutory powers — especially those bearing on individual rights — ought to be exercised reasonably, and an authority that fails this duty acts outside the scope of its authority; a regulation whose unreasonableness goes to the root of the matter is deemed to exceed its maker's authority and is null and void. (Transcribed from print; pending verification against the official report.)
