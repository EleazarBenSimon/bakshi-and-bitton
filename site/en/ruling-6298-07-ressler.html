HCJ 6298/07

Yehuda Ressler et al. v. The Knesset

Six unelected justices overrode the Knesset, striking down the Tal Law 6-3 and ruling that the conscription arrangement for yeshiva students enacted by the elected legislature was a disproportionate violation of equality under Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty. President Beinisch wrote the majority; Deputy President Grunis dissented. It was the first time the Court annulled a major social-policy choice of the people's representatives.

9 justicesVote 6–3Lead opinion: Dorit Beinisch
Why this matters
Here the power born in Bank Mizrahi descended from abstraction to a live policy choice. Where constitutional review had largely touched structure and authority, the Court here struck down 6-3 a contested social arrangement shaped by the people's representatives — who serves and who is deferred — in the name of a disproportionate violation of equality under Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty. A decision on this fractured dispute, which the elected legislature had taken upon itself through explicit legislation, was lifted from it and handed to six unelected justices. Proportionality — itself a judicial creation — became here the instrument by which a value-laden choice of the Knesset is measured and voided.
