Declarative

HCJ 54321-03-25

Movement for Quality (Abolition of) Government in Israel et al. v. Government of Israel et al. (dismissal of ISA Director Ronen Bar)

An unelected court claimed the power to decide who may head the Shin Bet: on 21 May 2025, 2-1, it voided the elected government's decision to dismiss ISA Director Ronen Bar as unlawful — no advisory committee, the PM in a conflict of interest, no factual basis, no hearing. Though Bar had already announced his resignation and the matter was moot, the majority chose to rule on principle and bind the government going forward rather than let it go; it granted no operative remedy. Sohlberg would have stopped at mootness.

3 justicesVote 2–1Lead opinion: Yitzhak Amit, Daphne Barak-Erez
Why this matters
Here the power entered the security room. The elected government decided to end the tenure of the Shin Bet director — and the Court ruled, 2-1, that the decision was unlawful, on grounds of conflict of interest and a flawed process. Even after Director Bar had announced his resignation and the matter was moot, the majority chose not to leave it there but to rule on principle and bind the government going forward — while Deputy President Sohlberg held it was enough that the petitions had become moot. The split was not over the legality of the dismissal on its merits but over the very willingness to seize the call: who is fit to head the security service, a question taken from the voters' representatives and handed to unelected justices.

Notes

Declaratory ruling; no operative remedy because Director Bar had announced (28 April 2025) that he would step down. The majority (President Amit and Justice Barak-Erez) held the dismissal unlawful; Deputy President Sohlberg agreed the process 'raised substantial difficulty' but regarded the petitions as having become moot following Bar's resignation announcement — so the 2-1 split is on whether to issue a principled ruling, not on the merits of legality.
