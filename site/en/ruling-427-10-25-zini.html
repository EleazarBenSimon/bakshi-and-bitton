HCJ 427/10/25 ובג"ץ 453/10/25 (מאוחדות)

Movement for Quality (Abolition of) Government in Israel et al. v. Government of Israel et al.

This time the Court chose not to step in: by 2-1 it dismissed seven petitions and let David Zini's appointment as Shin Bet chief stand — an appointment AG Baharav-Miara had branded "invalid and unlawful" over Netanyahu's conflict of interest, his aides being targets of the Shin Bet's Qatargate probes. Sohlberg and Mintz ruled that once Netanyahu and the AG agreed on a mechanism applied by the Gronis Committee, judges would not touch it absent exceptional cases. Only President Amit, in dissent, would have sent the matter back to test the Prime Minister's own conflict — and he lost.

3 justicesVote 2–1Lead opinion: Noam Sohlberg, David Mintz

HCJ 427/10/25 ובג"ץ 453/10/25 (מאוחדות) is a ruling of the Supreme Court of Israel, decided 2025-12-28, by a panel of 3 justices. Outcome: Dismissed. Vote: 2–1.

Why this matters
A counter-example only in its bottom line. The elected government's choice to head the Shin Bet was branded 'invalid and unlawful' in advance by the unelected Attorney General — who held that the Prime Minister was barred from making the appointment at all — and then forced through a 'compromise mechanism,' a vetting committee, and seven petitions before it could take effect. Even then the Court let it stand by only 2-1, the Court's own President dissenting and a single vote from reopening it. That the elected choice survived measures not judicial restraint but how abnormal the baseline has become: a security appointment now reaches the bench by default, one vote from being taken out of elected hands.
