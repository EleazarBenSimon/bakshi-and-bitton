HCJ 1308/17

Silwad Municipality et al. v. The Knesset et al.

Eight unelected justices to one erased a statute the Knesset had enacted: the Settlements Regularization Law, which let the state retroactively expropriate privately-owned Palestinian land to legalize Israeli settlement built on it. In the lead opinion President Hayut ruled the law a disproportionate breach of the owners' property and dignity. Tellingly, the Attorney General refused to defend it at all, leaving the Knesset's own counsel to argue for it alone. Justice Sohlberg dissented.

9 justicesVote 8–1Lead opinion: Esther Hayut

HCJ 1308/17 is a ruling of the Supreme Court of Israel, decided 2020-06-09, by a panel of 9 justices. Outcome: Struck down. Vote: 8–1.

Why this matters
Here the authority born in Bank Mizrahi was wielded at full weight: by a majority of eight unelected justices to one, the Court erased a statute the Knesset had enacted, in the name of a constitutional supremacy it had proclaimed for itself and a "proportionality" measured against a Basic Law. The structural anomaly is the collapse of the statute's defense: the Attorney General refused to defend the law at all, leaving the elected legislature to argue for it alone — against the very office meant to represent the executive. Judicial power was thus set not only above the Knesset but against it, with the executive's professional arm already lined up on the side of annulment.
