HCJ 1892/14

Association for Civil Rights in Israel et al. v. Minister of Public Security

Three unelected justices, unanimously, seized control of the prison system's budget priorities: invoking "reasonableness," they ordered the Minister of Public Security and the Prison Service to deliver the 4 m²-per-prisoner standard within 18 months — a standard left on paper while actual space sat at roughly 3.1 m² for decades. Here reasonableness review was stretched past a single decision onto sustained administrative inaction, casting the Court as de facto planner of the executive branch.

3 justicesVote 3–0Lead opinion: Elyakim Rubinstein
Why this matters
Here reasonableness shifted from reviewing decisions to planning a budget. Reasonableness review had been aimed at a single administrative decision; here three unelected justices, unanimously, applied it to sustained inaction — decades of non-implementation of a regulation — and ordered the Minister and the Prison Service to deliver, within 18 months, a standard that means allocating resources and priorities across the prison system. In doing so the Court took into its own hands the budgetary priorities of the executive, a choice that by nature belongs to the elected tier and those answerable to it for the budget. That the deadline passed without full implementation only underscores the limit of the power the Court claimed for itself: to command an outcome while the means of execution remain with the one who was elected.
