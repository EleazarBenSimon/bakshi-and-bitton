HCJ 7146/12

Adam et al. v. The Knesset et al.

Nine unelected justices unanimously voided a law the Knesset had passed: Amendment No. 3 to the Prevention of Infiltration Law, which let asylum seekers be detained up to three years without a concrete judicial proceeding. Invoking "proportionality" and Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty, the Court placed its own judgment above the people's elected representatives and ordered detainees released within 90 days — a policy the Knesset set, the justices erased.

9 justicesVote 9–0

HCJ 7146/12 is a ruling of the Supreme Court of Israel, decided 2013-09-16, by a panel of 9 justices. Outcome: Struck down. Vote: 9–0.

Why this matters
Here the tool born in Bank Mizrahi was actually deployed against a real statute: nine unelected justices unanimously voided an amendment the Knesset had passed, without a single dissenting voice. The choice of how to confront the phenomenon of infiltration — a quintessential policy question the voters' representatives had settled by law — was lifted out of the political arena and re-decided by the justices in the name of "proportionality" and Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty. The Court did not stop at annulment but dictated the remedy too, ordering the detainees released within ninety days. And when the Knesset sought to reclaim the decision and legislated a revised scheme, it opened a sequence in which every renewed attempt was dragged back before the same court.
