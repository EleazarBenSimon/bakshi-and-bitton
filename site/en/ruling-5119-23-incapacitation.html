Partially struck

HCJ 5119/23

Movement for Quality (Abolition of) Government in Israel et al. v. The Knesset et al.

By a single vote (6-5), eleven unelected justices claimed authority to police how the elected Knesset wields its constituent power: they ruled that Amendment No. 12 to Basic Law: The Government (the Incapacitation Law) was an 'abuse' of that power because of a personal purpose tied to the incumbent Prime Minister. Rather than strike it, the Court invented a remedy: it deferred the amendment's application until after the next Knesset elections. Justices Sohlberg, Mintz, Willner, Stein, and Kasher dissented. The 'personal legislation' test was one the Court itself created in HCJ 5969/20 Shafir.

11 justicesVote 6–5Lead opinion: Esther Hayut
Why this matters
Here the review reached past the content of a law to the Knesset's very exercise of its constituent power — over an amendment to a Basic Law, the highest tier of legislation. By a single vote (6 to 5), eleven unelected justices declared Amendment No. 12 to Basic Law: The Government an 'abuse' of constituent power, under a 'personal legislation' test the Court itself had forged in the HCJ 5969/20 Shafir line — a yardstick no one legislated. And rather than strike it, the Court seized a power further still: it deferred the amendment's effective date by its own hand until after the next elections, rewriting a Basic Law in place of the body that enacted it. Even the five dissenters who would have left it standing could not halt the transfer of the decision over a Basic Law's validity from the elected constituent legislature to a bench the Court assembled for itself.

Notes

Decided the same day as HCJ 5658/23 (reasonableness amendment). Justices Hayut and Baron participated within the statutory three-month post-retirement window per §15(d) Basic Law: The Judiciary and §10A Courts Law (both retired October 2023). The remedy of delayed-effect interpretation (rather than outright striking) was novel; it represents a 'partial strike' in operational terms. Doctrinally builds on HCJ 5969/20 Shafir's 'abuse of constituent power' framework, importing the 'personal legislation' refinement first articulated in the same line. Panel composition reflects the eleven-justice expanded bench specified for the incapacitation petitions, distinct from the fifteen-justice en banc panel convened for HCJ 5658/23.
