Partially struck

HCJ 8665/14

Desta et al. v. The Knesset et al.

Nine unelected justices, led by President Naor, didn't just void Amendment No. 5 to the Prevention of Infiltration Law - they swapped the Knesset's judgment for their own. Where the legislature set a 20-month residence cap at the Holot Center, the Court simply picked a different number: 12 months. Invoking 'proportionality,' it ordered everyone past the new cap released within 15 days and commanded the Knesset to rewrite its statute to match.

9 justicesLead opinion: Miriam Naor

HCJ 8665/14 is a ruling of the Supreme Court of Israel, decided 2015-08-11, by a panel of 9 justices. Outcome: Partially struck.

Why this matters
Here the Court was not content with the power to strike a law down — it took for itself the power to decide what the law should have said. In voiding Amendment No. 5 to the Prevention of Infiltration Law it did not leave the redesign to the Knesset; in the name of "proportionality" it replaced the legislature's twenty-month residence cap with a number of its own — twelve months — and ordered everyone past the line it had drawn released within fifteen days. This is the third in a sequence that had already struck down its predecessors (Adam 2013, Eitan 2014): not review of a limit the legislature crossed, but the active setting of the limit itself, as though the justices were the drafters — a power taken from the elected Knesset and never granted to the Court.
