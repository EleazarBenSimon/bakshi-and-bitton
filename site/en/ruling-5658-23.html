Struck down

HCJ 5658/23

Movement for Quality (Abolition of) Government in Israel et al. v. The Knesset et al.

For the first time in Israeli history, the Supreme Court voided an amendment to a Basic Law. Sitting en banc with all 15 justices and splitting 8-7, the Court struck down Amendment No. 3 to Basic Law: The Judiciary — the amendment by which the elected Knesset removed the reasonableness ground of review over decisions of the government, Prime Minister, and ministers. In the same breath, by 12-3, this unelected court claimed for itself the power to review and annul amendments to the Basic Laws themselves — the last ceiling that had constrained it.

15 justicesVote 8–7Lead opinion: Esther Hayut, Uzi Vogelman, Yitzhak Amit, Daphne Barak-Erez, Anat Baron, Ofer Grosskopf, Khaled Kabub, Ruth Ronen

HCJ 5658/23 is a ruling of the Supreme Court of Israel, decided 2024-01-01, by a panel of 15 justices. Outcome: Struck down. Vote: 8–7.

Why this matters
This is the last ceiling, breached. Until now the Court had struck only "ordinary" statutes; for the first time it voided an amendment to a Basic Law — and in the same breath reserved for itself, 12-3, the power to review and annul the Basic Laws themselves. From this point there is no law the Knesset can pass that the Court has not reserved the right to erase — including a law meant to narrow the Court's own power. An elected majority of 64 was not enough.

Notes

First instance in Israeli history of the Supreme Court striking down an amendment to a Basic Law. Justices Hayut and Baron had retired in October 2023 (Hayut Oct 16, Baron Oct 12) but participated in the ruling within the statutory three-month post-retirement window per §15(d) Basic Law: The Judiciary and §10A Courts Law. The 12-3 holding on the principle of reviewing Basic Laws was decided concurrently with the 8-7 holding on this specific amendment. Justices Willner, Stein, and Canfy-Steinitz accepted the review-power principle but voted against application by reading the amendment narrowly to preserve 'reasonableness-balancing' per HCJ 389/80 Dapei Zahav.
