Warning of voidness

HCJ 5969/20

Stav Shafir et al. v. The Knesset et al.

A nine-justice panel of the Supreme Court claimed a new power: to police Basic-Law amendments enacted by the elected Knesset. It declined to strike the temporary amendments to Basic Law: The State Economy and Basic Law: The Knesset, but invented a 'warning of voidness' — by 6 to 3, led by President Hayut (Sohlberg, Mintz, and Elron dissenting), it decreed that similar future amendments would be void. With that it conjured the tests of 'abuse of constituent power,' generality, and constitutional compatibility — a doctrine spun from nothing, later applied in HCJ 5658/23 and HCJ 5119/23.

9 justicesVote 6–3Lead opinion: Esther Hayut, Neal Hendel, Yitzhak Amit, Daphne Barak-Erez, Anat Baron, George Karra
Why this matters
Here the Court claimed the power without yet exercising it — which is exactly what made it hard to challenge. It struck nothing down: the temporary amendments to the Basic Laws were left standing. But sitting nine, by 6 to 3 under President Hayut, it invented a 'warning of voidness' and conjured the tests of 'abuse of constituent power' out of nothing — and declared for the first time that it may police even Basic-Law amendments enacted by the elected Knesset. Because nothing was actually voided, no losing party remained to attack the innovation itself; and once the doctrine matured it was applied in HCJ 5658/23 and HCJ 5119/23. This is where the groundwork was laid — the authority to sit in judgment over the very validity of the Basic Laws, an authority the Knesset never granted.

Notes

Doctrinal anchor case. Establishes the tripartite test (abuse of constituent power, generality, compatibility with the constitutional fabric) subsequently invoked in HCJ 5658/23 (reasonableness amendment, January 2024) and HCJ 5119/23 (incapacitation amendment, January 2024). Disposition is a 'warning of voidness' (התראת בטלות) — not a strike — the Court declined to void the amendments but signaled future similar amendments would be void under the abuse-of-constituent-power test. Vote: 6-3, uniform across doctrine and disposition. Majority: Hayut (lead), Hendel, Amit, Barak-Erez, Baron, Karra. Dissent: Sohlberg, Mintz, Elron. Internal divergence within the 6-justice majority (Hayut's analytical framework for identifying abuse-of-constituent-power vs. Barak-Erez's framework) did not produce a separate vote tally. The petitioner is recorded as 'Party' to reflect Shafir's status as a former Knesset Member; consolidated petitions included NGO co-petitioners. An earlier draft of this record encoded the panel and vote incorrectly (7-2 with Melcer, Vogelman, Willner listed) per a research brief that omitted Justice Elron's dissent and confused the panel composition with an unrelated sibling-era ruling; corrected against arnontl.com legal-firm analysis, afiklaw.com docket header, and Verfassungsblog summary.
